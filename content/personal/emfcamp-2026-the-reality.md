I had an incredible time at EMFCamp. Likely one of the best times of my life.

I've written about that [feeling of euphoria you get](./emfcamp-2026-the-reality.md).

Everyone I've spoken to said they want to go.

But I also think it's important to mention the things that didn't go so well. Because if you're reading this and thinking you want to go. I think you need to understand what you also should expect.

## Overwhealming

I would love to say EMFCamp is this idealic weekend. But I did meet people who were not having a good time.

It is an overwhealming place. There is constant noise and lights, which some people might not be prepared for.

EMFCamp does have quiet areas, but it's not easy to migrate to if you discover you're in a noisy end of the field. I'm sure if you asked someone they would be more than willing to help though.

## Imposter Syndrome

Several people I spoke to also mentioned imposter syndrome.

There's so many incredibly talented people, professional and hobbiest, doing amazing things that it's daunting when you turn up (like me) with a cheap LED cape that you got off eBay and reverse engineered.

But also everyone is incredibly friendly. I went to a talk about movie props and had a lovelly chat to the speaker in the bar about it later, probably a bit half cut myself, I'd just had my first BuzzBall which was delivered out of a cyber-punk style tube.

But you probably have something that other people don't know how to do, and EMFCamp is all about sharing those skills.

It's also worth keeping in mind that the people who are showing off the cool stuff they're doing also won't be skilled in other things too.

## Conflict

I was on the receiving end of a derisive shushing when attending the Q&A tent after a talk, and the people who were already there from a different talk were trying to listen to someone who had lost their voice.

I think there was space to find an amacable solution. But there was no attendant to limit time, or suggest we find another space and direct stragglers to where we had gone.

This is something I've fedback to the EMFCamp organisers and I'm confident they will come up with a better solution that I haven't thought of.

## Liberalist

 It's worth being familiar with the [Code of Conduct](https://www.emfcamp.org/code-of-conduct/) before buying your tickets. If you feel like anything there is going to find difficult, this event is likely not for you.

 There's absolutely a librialist slant and if you start being exclusionary you'll likely get shouted down, or shunned pretty fast.

It's worth noting if you're uncomfortable with any of the spectrum of the rainbow flag, and further, you might not have a good time.

Or the other way to look at it is you'll also get to meet people who you wouldn't otherwise meet, and chat and maybe find that you get along.

## Unintended Damage

I had a kid (8-ish) run up and pull on my LED cape without asking, which was wrapped around my neck. I also saw similar complaints about handsy kids on social media.

There's a huge neuro-diverse range at EMFCamp from adults and kids, and kids are prone to being over-stimulated and not know how to handle the unusual situations, that EMFCamp will throw at them. This is bound to cause damage and upset on both sides.

Everything I saw and heard about was resolved, but you might want consider anything that you bring to EMFCamp to expect it to end up a bit broken, or to consider how to keep people away from your stuff if it's delicate, or if you don't fancy being assertive about not sharing (which is perfectly reasonable).
